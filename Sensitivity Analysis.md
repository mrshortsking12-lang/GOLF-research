# Sensitivity Analysis

Prepared September 29, 2026. Company: Acushnet Holdings Corp. (NYSE: GOLF).

## Scenario inputs

The two independent scenario inputs are golf-club revenue growth and golf-ball revenue growth. They extend the existing company-wide DCF using a product-sales split; the original model does not forecast these categories separately. The 5% base is the existing forward DCF default, not the reverse DCF’s solved growth rate.

| Driver | Lower | Base | Higher | Units and affected years | Range rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| Club-sales growth | 3% | 5% | 7% | Annual revenue growth, same rate in each of Years 1–5 | Labeled judgment: test weaker or stronger launch demand, replacement purchases, pricing, and market share. |
| Golf-ball sales growth | 3% | 5% | 7% | Annual revenue growth, same rate in each of Years 1–5 | Labeled judgment: test weaker or stronger golf participation, replacement demand, pricing, and market share. |

The changes are **−2 or +2 percentage points**, not relative changes of 2%. Historical 2025 category growth was 7.5% for clubs and 4.4% for balls; the scenario ranges are judgments, not management guidance. These are revenue-growth inputs, not unit-volume forecasts.

Change one driver at a time. Other product sales grow at 5% in every explicit forecast year. All products revert to 2.5% annual growth in the terminal period; the larger or smaller Year-5 sales base remains. Years 1–5 are normalized forward periods, not specific calendar years.

## Sales allocation and unchanged assumptions

- Starting company revenue: **$2,662.5 million**, the existing model’s normalized 2026 guidance-midpoint run rate.
- Clubs: 2025 revenue of **$775.2 million / $2,558.730 million = 30.30%** of company sales.
- Balls: 2025 revenue of **$821.0 million / $2,558.730 million = 32.09%** of company sales.
- EBIT margins, Years 1–5: **12.50%, 12.75%, 13.00%, 13.25%, 13.50%**; terminal margin: **13.50%**.
- Tax: **23%**; D&A: **2.2% of sales**; physical capex: **3.5% of sales**.
- Additional working capital: **20% of incremental sales**; net ERP investment: **$25, $15, $10, $5, $5 million** in Years 1–5.
- WACC: **8.5%**; terminal growth: **2.5%**; end-of-year discounting.
- Existing equity bridge and approximately **58.58 million diluted shares** remain fixed.

Amounts below are **USD millions**, except **USD per share**. Free cash flow consistently means **FCFF**, available to debt and equity investors.

## How each valuation driver links to stock value

The two valuation drivers are **club-sales growth** and **golf-ball sales growth**. Stock value here means the DCF’s estimated intrinsic value per share. A change in this estimate is not a guaranteed change in the market trading price. The comparison starts from the model’s **$54.20 per share** base, not a live stock quote.

For each driver, the calculation links operating performance to shareholder value:

```text
Category growth → company sales → EBIT → after-tax operating profit
→ FCFF after reinvestment → discounted business value → equity value / shares

Estimated stock value = (PV of Years 1–5 FCFF + PV of terminal value
                         − debt + cash − other claims) / diluted shares

Change in estimated stock value = change in business value / 58.582333 million shares
```

Debt, cash, other claims, shares, WACC, margins, and investment ratios remain fixed between scenarios. Therefore, changes in business value flow dollar-for-dollar into equity value before division by shares. The sensitivity reflects both explicit forecast cash flows and terminal cash flows, not Year-5 profit alone.

| 5% | $54.20 | +0.00 | +0.00% | +0.00 |
| 7% | $55.95 | +1.75 | +3.22% | +102.35 |

Increasing growth from 5% to 7% raises estimated stock value by **$1.75 per share**. Reducing growth from 5% to 3% lowers estimated stock value by **$1.63 per share**.

Across the tested 3%–7% range, the average sensitivity is **$0.84 per share for each 1-percentage-point change** in annual category growth, calculated as `($55.95 − $52.57) / 4 percentage points` using unrounded outputs. This is a range-based approximation; compounding makes the relationship nonlinear.

## Why the drivers move profit, cash flow, and value

**Higher growth:** Sales compound from a larger category revenue base, increasing EBIT at unchanged margins. More sales also require inventory, working capital, and capital spending. In the 7% scenarios, FCFF falls below base in Years 1–2 before rising above base in Years 3–5. The larger final-year revenue base raises terminal cash flow and modeled value.

**Lower growth:** Lower category sales reduce EBIT and the long-term cash-flow base, decreasing modeled value. Reduced working-capital and capital-spending needs initially cushion cash flow: the 3% scenarios have higher FCFF than base in Years 1–2, then lower FCFF in Years 3–5.

**Balls versus clubs:** The same growth shift has a slightly larger effect for balls because their starting share of sales is larger. The model uses the same company-wide economics for both categories; this result does not establish that balls have better actual margins or returns on investment.

**Compounding:** The positive and negative effects are not exactly symmetric because 7% and 3% growth compound differently around the 5% base.

## Reproduction

For selected category sales weight `w`, category growth `g`, and forecast year `t`:

```text
Revenue_t = 2,662.5 × [(1 − w) × 1.05^t + w × (1 + g)^t]
EBIT_t = Revenue_t × EBIT_margin_t
FCFF_t = EBIT_t × (1 − 23%) + D&A_t − physical_capex_t
         − 20% × (Revenue_t − Revenue_(t−1)) − net_ERP_t
```

Terminal FCFF is rebuilt at 2.5% growth using terminal margins and reinvestment. Value per share equals discounted explicit FCFF plus discounted terminal value, less the existing debt/cash/other-claims bridge, divided by the existing diluted-share proxy.

Run from the workspace root:

```bash
python3 GOLF/valuation/product_growth_sensitivity.py
```

The script verifies that allocating product sales while keeping category growth at 5% reproduces the original DCF’s revenue, EBIT, FCFF, and value per share. Results are calculated before rounding.
