# Sensitivity Analysis

Acushnet Holdings Corp. (NYSE: GOLF). Updated September 29, 2026.

## Updated forecast basis

**The model now projects club sales at 8.9% annual growth and golf-ball sales at 5.4%, replacing the shared 5% assumption.** Both rates are arithmetic averages of four full-year reported growth observations, 2022–2025. Forecasting those averages for Years 1–5 remains a judgment, not company guidance.

| Full year | Club-sales growth | Golf-ball sales growth | Source |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 2022 | 10.5% | 1.7% | [K22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201323000011/golf-20221231.htm) |
| 2023 | 8.1% | 12.2% | [K24](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201325000009/golf-20241231.htm) |
| 2024 | 9.5% | 3.3% | [K24](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201325000009/golf-20241231.htm) |
| 2025 | 7.5% | 4.4% | [K25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm) |
| **Four-year arithmetic average** | **8.9%** | **5.4%** | Calculated from the four published rates |

```text
Club average = (10.5% + 8.1% + 9.5% + 7.5%) / 4 = 8.9%
Ball average = (1.7% + 12.2% + 3.3% + 4.4%) / 4 = 5.4%
```

These are reported USD revenue growth rates, including price, volume, and currency effects. The calculation uses published rates rounded to one decimal percentage point; it is not a CAGR or an average of sales dollars. Four annual growth observations require a 2021 comparison base for the 2022 observation. Partial-year 2026 results are excluded.

The 2024 filing recasts 2023 club sales to $658.7 million and growth to 8.1%, versus $658.6 million and 8.0% in the original 2023 report. This model uses the later presentation for comparability. The historical segment reorganization and rounding mean the average should not be treated as an exact organic-growth estimate.

## Link from each driver to estimated stock value

```text
Category growth → revenue → EBIT → after-tax operating profit
→ FCFF after reinvestment → discounted business value → equity value / shares

Value/share = (PV of Years 1–5 FCFF + PV of terminal value
               − debt + cash − other claims) / diluted shares
Change in value/share = change in business value / 58.582333 million shares
```

Debt, cash, other claims, and shares stay fixed across scenarios, so a change in business value also changes equity value by the same amount. These are estimated intrinsic stock values, not predictions of changes in the traded stock price.

### Golf clubs growth

- Lower growth, **6.9%**: estimated stock value **$56.21**, a change of **$-1.87 per share**. Business/equity value changes by **-109.51 million USD**.
- Base growth, **8.9%**: estimated stock value **$58.08**.
- Higher growth, **10.9%**: estimated stock value **$60.09**, a change of **$+2.00 per share**. Business/equity value changes by **+117.45 million USD**.

Across this tested four-percentage-point range, average sensitivity is **$0.97 per share per 1-percentage-point change** in annual category growth. It equals `(higher value − lower value) / 4` using unrounded outputs, not a constant derivative outside the tested range.

### Golf balls growth

- Lower growth, **3.4%**: estimated stock value **$56.34**, a change of **$-1.75 per share**. Business/equity value changes by **-102.31 million USD**.
- Base growth, **5.4%**: estimated stock value **$58.08**.
- Higher growth, **7.4%**: estimated stock value **$59.96**, a change of **$+1.88 per share**. Business/equity value changes by **+109.96 million USD**.

Across this tested four-percentage-point range, average sensitivity is **$0.91 per share per 1-percentage-point change** in annual category growth. It equals `(higher value − lower value) / 4` using unrounded outputs, not a constant derivative outside the tested range.

Higher growth compounds sales and EBIT but also consumes cash through inventory, other working capital, and capital spending. Lower growth reduces profit while initially freeing some cash from reinvestment. The terminal slowdown lowers incremental working-capital needs and preserves the larger or smaller final-year revenue base, affecting terminal value. Both explicit and terminal cash flows contribute to the valuation changes.

The effects of +2 and −2 percentage points are not exactly symmetric because sales compound. In this updated model, clubs can have a larger valuation sensitivity despite a smaller starting sales weight because their higher base growth compounds more quickly. Company-wide margins and investment ratios are applied to both categories; these results do not demonstrate actual category-specific profitability.

## Reproduction and checks

For Year t, club weight wc, ball weight wb, club growth gc, and ball growth gb:

```text
Revenue_t = 2,662.5 × [wc × (1 + gc)^t + wb × (1 + gb)^t
                      + (1 − wc − wb) × 1.05^t]
EBIT_t = Revenue_t × EBIT_margin_t
FCFF_t = EBIT_t × 77% + D&A_t − capex_t
         − 20% × (Revenue_t − Revenue_(t−1)) − net_ERP_t
```

Run from the workspace root:

```bash
python3 GOLF/valuation/product_growth_sensitivity.py
python3 GOLF/valuation/write_sensitivity_report.py
```

The default [dcf.py](dcf.py) now uses the two historical-average category rates. [product_growth_sensitivity.py](product_growth_sensitivity.py) changes one category at a time. Passing an explicit uniform growth rate to the DCF retains the earlier uniform-growth mode for comparison and the dated reverse DCF. Checks confirm the unchanged uniform 5% valuation, original reverse-price solution, base-scenario agreement, equity bridge, and lower/base/higher valuation ordering.
