## What it is and how it makes money

Acushnet is a global golf-products company built mainly around two premium brands: ([10-K, Item 1, “Overview”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

- Titleist, which sells golf equipment like golf balls, clubs, wedges, and putters.
- FootJoy, which sells golf apparel and wearables like shoes, gloves, apparel, and outerwear.

The company also sells golf bags, travel products, headwear, and other accessories through its Golf Gear segment and other brands, including KJUS, Club Glove, and Links & Kings. ([10-K, Item 1, “Our Products”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

Acushnet makes money by designing, manufacturing or sourcing, marketing, and distributing golf products. It sells through on-course golf shops, specialty golf retailers, other qualified retailers, distributors, and its own e-commerce sites. Its main customer is the dedicated golfer—a player willing to spend time and money on performance products and improving their game. ([10-K, Item 1, “Overview,” “Dedicated Golfers,” and “Sales and Distribution”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

Management describes its marketing model as a “pyramid of influence.” Professional golfers use Titleist and FootJoy products, reinforcing the brands' performance reputation and encouraging amateur golfers to buy them with the idea that their game could mimic the pros with the same equipment. In 2025, Titleist balls represented 72% of all golf balls used on worldwide professional tours. ([10-K, Item 1, “Dedicated Golfers” and “Titleist Golf Balls”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

The product mix is important:

- Nearly 40% of 2025 sales came from consumable products, mainly golf balls and gloves. These products require replacement and may create repeat purchases. ([10-K, Item 1, “Product Platform”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))
- More than 60% of sales came from durable products such as clubs, shoes, apparel, and gear. These products have higher purchase prices but longer replacement cycles. ([10-K, Item 1, “Product Platform”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

Acushnet reports three main operating segments:

## What is good

### Strong brands and category leadership

Titleist and FootJoy have long-standing positions in golf. Titleist has been the leading ball on professional tours for decades, while FootJoy holds leading positions in golf shoes and gloves. The conclusion that this reputation may support trust, pricing, and repeat purchasing is analysis rather than a reported financial fact. ([10-K, Item 1, “Overview,” “Titleist Golf Balls,” and “FootJoy”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Exposure to committed customers

Acushnet focuses on dedicated golfers rather than casual consumers. Management believes these customers are consistent purchasers who invest in premium performance products. The conclusion that this focus may make demand more resilient is analysis. ([10-K, Item 1, “Dedicated Golfers”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Favorable participation trends

Management reported that worldwide rounds played increased approximately 2% in 2025 and 22% compared with 2019. U.S. rounds increased approximately 1% in 2025 and 25% compared with 2019. The 10-K notes that company-cited industry data were not independently verified. ([10-K, “Industry and Market Data”; Item 7, “Rounds of Play”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Continued sales growth

Net sales rose in each year presented, increasing from $2.382 billion in 2023 to $2.457 billion in 2024 and $2.559 billion in 2025. Titleist equipment sales increased 5.9% as reported in 2025, helped by higher club prices, the 2025 Pro V1 launch, T-Series irons, and GT hybrids. Golf Gear sales grew 5.5% as reported, driven by higher average selling prices. ([10-K, Item 7, “Results of Operations” and “Segment Results”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Adequate near-term liquidity and improved debt maturity

At year-end, Acushnet reported $48.7 million of unrestricted cash, $514.7 million available under its revolving credit facility, and another $37.8 million available under local facilities. The company refinanced its debt in late 2025 by issuing $500 million of 5.625% senior notes due 2033 and extending its revolving facility to 2030. Management reported compliance with the credit agreement and note-indenture covenants. ([10-K, Item 7, “Liquidity and Capital Resources” and “Debt and Financing Arrangements”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Effective financial reporting controls

PwC issued unqualified opinions on Acushnet's financial statements and internal control over financial reporting as of December 31, 2025. ([10-K, Item 8, “Report of Independent Registered Public Accounting Firm”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

## What is bad

### Sales growth did not produce comparable profit growth

Revenue increased 4.1% in 2025, but operating income declined 1.6% and net income attributable to Acushnet declined 12.0%. Adjusted EBITDA increased only 1.5%, while adjusted EBITDA margin fell from 16.5% to 16.0%. The percentage changes are calculated from the filed amounts. ([10-K, Item 7, “Results of Operations”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Titleist's operating income declined despite higher sales

Titleist equipment is Acushnet's largest segment. Its sales rose 5.9%, but segment operating income fell $29.0 million, or 10.6%. MD&A attributes this to higher selling, advertising, R&D, and administrative expenses, as well as tariff and manufacturing costs. The final sentence—that pressure in the largest segment deserves particular attention—is analytical judgment. ([10-K, Item 7, “Titleist Golf Equipment Segment”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Operating cash flow weakened

Operating cash flow declined from $371.8 million in 2023 to $245.1 million in 2024 and $194.4 million in 2025. Management attributed the latest decline mainly to increased ERP investment and other working-capital requirements. The company also spent $74.3 million on capital expenditures in 2025. ([10-K, Item 7, “Cash Flows” and “Capital Expenditures and Other Investments”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

A simple cash-flow calculation—operating cash flow less capital expenditures—was **$120.0 million** in 2025, compared with $170.5 million in 2024 and $270.3 million in 2023. The calculations use the filed amounts of $194.370 million less $74.342 million, $245.108 million less $74.624 million, and $371.827 million less $101.486 million, respectively. This is an author calculation, not a company-reported free-cash-flow measure. ([10-K, Item 7, “Cash Flows”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Capital returns exceeded internally generated cash

Acushnet repurchased $211.5 million of stock and paid $56.2 million in dividends during 2025. Together, these uses exceeded both operating cash flow and the author-calculated operating cash flow less capital expenditures. Part of the repurchase activity involved buying $125.0 million of shares from controlling shareholder Magnus Holdings. The final sentence is an analytical conclusion, not management guidance. ([10-K, Item 7, “Dividends and Share Repurchase Program”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### FootJoy sales remained weak

FootJoy sales declined 0.8% in 2025, from $574.6 million to $569.9 million, after declining from $590.0 million in 2023 to $574.6 million in 2024. In 2025, lower sales volumes, primarily in footwear, more than offset higher average prices across all product categories. Segment operating income improved 14.0%, driven by pricing and favorable mix, partially offset by lower volume and incremental tariff costs. ([10-K, Item 1, “FootJoy”; Item 7, “FootJoy Golf Wear Segment”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

## Challenges and risks

### Tariffs and international supply chains

Acushnet manufactures golf balls and gloves in Thailand, sources substantially all FootJoy footwear from Vietnam, and depends on other international suppliers. Tariffs increased costs in 2025, including within all three reportable segments. Future trade restrictions could raise product costs, disrupt shipping, or force price increases that reduce demand. ([10-K, Item 1, “Manufacturing” and “Raw Materials and Components”; Item 1A, trade-policy risk; Item 7, segment results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Foreign-exchange exposure

More than 40% of sales were generated outside the United States in each of 2023–2025. Substantially all such sales were generated in applicable local currencies, while approximately 80% of cost of goods sold incurred by subsidiaries outside the United States was denominated in U.S. dollars. Acushnet hedges currency risk, but hedging reduces rather than eliminates earnings volatility. ([10-K, Item 7, “Foreign Currency”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Discretionary consumer demand

Golf equipment and apparel are discretionary purchases. Inflation, interest rates, consumer confidence, employment conditions, and asset values can affect purchases. ([10-K, Item 7, “Economic Conditions”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Weather and seasonality

Golf participation depends on playable weather. Acushnet normally earns a larger portion of its sales and profit in the first half of the year. Poor spring or summer weather can reduce rounds, retail sell-through, reorders, and demand for consumable products. ([10-K, Item 7, “Weather Conditions” and “Seasonality”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Product-launch cycles

Most major Titleist golf-ball and golf-club product families follow two-year launch cycles, although specific timing differs by product. New models increase initial retailer orders, while announcements can cause customers to delay purchases of older products. Results can therefore fluctuate with launch timing. ([10-K, Item 7, “Cyclicality” and “Product Life Cycles”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Brand and competitive risk

Acushnet's advantage depends heavily on the reputation of Titleist and FootJoy. Product-quality failures, weak innovation, unsuccessful launches, or stronger competing products could damage pricing power and market share. ([10-K, Item 1A, brand, competition, innovation, and product-quality risks](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Debt and controlling-shareholder influence

Debt creates fixed interest obligations and can restrict dividends, repurchases, investments, and acquisitions. Magnus controls a majority of voting power and can strongly influence board membership, dividends, strategy, and related transactions. Its interests may differ from those of minority shareholders. ([10-K, Item 1A, “Risks Related to Our Indebtedness” and controlled-company risks](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

### Execution risk from major investments

Acushnet is implementing a multi-year global cloud ERP system and changing parts of its supply chain. These projects may improve efficiency, but they also create cost-overrun, disruption, and implementation risks. ([10-K, Item 7, “Recent Developments”; Item 1A, ERP and supply-chain risks](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

## Advantages that may support long-term value

- Premium brands with long operating histories and strong professional-tour validation.
- A loyal target market of dedicated golfers.
- A mix of recurring consumables and higher-value durable equipment.
- Internal golf-ball manufacturing capabilities, product research, fitting networks, and relationships with premium golf retailers.
- Global distribution and direct-to-consumer channels.
- Product breadth that allows Acushnet to serve the same golfer across balls, clubs, shoes, gloves, apparel, and gear.
- Active currency hedging and efforts to diversify and strengthen the supply chain.

*Sources for the advantages listed above: [10-K, Item 1, “Overview,” “Our Core Focus,” “Research and Development,” “Manufacturing,” and “Sales and Distribution”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm), plus [Item 7, “Foreign Currency”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm). Whether these characteristics ultimately create investment value is analytical judgment.*

- The company expects operating cash flow, cash on hand, and available credit facilities to cover liquidity requirements for at least the next 12 months. ([10-K, Item 7, “Liquidity and Capital Resources”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))
- Management plans continued product launches, investment in fittings and consumer engagement, expansion in golf gear and wear, and growth in established international markets. ([10-K, Item 1, “Our Growth Strategies”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm))

Management's outlook is cautiously positive about golf demand, but it also points to another investment-heavy year. The central question is whether product launches, pricing, and participation can grow revenue fast enough to offset tariffs, operating expenses, ERP costs, capital expenditures, and working-capital needs. *This is analysis based on the [10-K's operating-factor and outlook disclosures](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1672013/000167201326000057/golf-20251231.htm).*

### Investment interpretation

The call strengthens the case that Titleist equipment demand and golf participation remain healthy.
